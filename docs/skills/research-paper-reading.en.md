<header class="component-header"><p class="component-kind">Skill</p><h1>research-paper-reading</h1><p>Read one scholarly paper with a human, from its final claim down to the section, paragraph, sentence, figure, table, and supplement that support it.</p></header>

<section class="component-section component-summary"><p>Use this Skill when you have a specific paper and want to understand it rather than merely summarize it. The paper stays a source: its claims are explained and tested against its own methods and evidence, not accepted automatically as facts.</p><div class="release-stamp"><span>Current public Release</span><strong><a href="https://github.com/chenhaoran2068/research-paper-reading/releases/tag/v0.3.0">v0.3.0</a></strong></div></section>

<section class="component-section"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">How to start</p><h2>Give the paper, then choose the depth</h2></div></div><div class="boundary-grid"><div><h3>Provide one source</h3><p>Attach a PDF, provide a stable link, title, DOI, abstract, or a specific passage. State whether the source may be downloaded or retained.</p></div><div><h3>Choose a reading depth</h3><p><code>full_close_reading</code> reads the whole accessible paper; <code>structured_close_reading</code> works through each substantive section; <code>focused_reading</code> stays with a named part.</p></div><div><h3>Add a lens when useful</h3><p>Method learning, terminology, figures, critical appraisal, Journal Club, and research-opportunity discussion can be added without replacing the reading sequence.</p></div></div></section>

<section class="component-section"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">Reading sequence</p><h2>Move from the whole paper to its evidence</h2></div></div><div class="reading-sequence"><article><span>01</span><div><h3>Identify the paper's final claim</h3><p>State the question, the answer the authors give, the evidence behind it, and the boundary of that answer. Understand the claim first; do not treat it as proved merely because the authors state it.</p></div></article><article><span>02</span><div><h3>Read section by section</h3><p>For every section, explain its job, the part of the question it addresses, and how it takes the paper toward its final claim.</p></div></article><article><span>03</span><div><h3>Read paragraph by paragraph</h3><p>Identify whether a paragraph supplies background, a definition, a method, a result, an interpretation, or a limitation; then explain its evidence and role in the section.</p></div></article><article><span>04</span><div><h3>Read key sentences</h3><p>Slow down for definitions, method conditions, numbers, comparisons, limitations, cautious language, and reasoning turns. Do not mechanically paraphrase empty connector sentences.</p></div></article><article><span>05</span><div><h3>Read figures, tables, and supplements one by one</h3><p>Locate each item in its paragraph, section, and claim. Then read its title, panels, axes, groups, colors, units, denominators, legend, and statistical marks; state what it supports and what it cannot establish.</p></div></article><article><span>06</span><div><h3>Check that the whole paper is understood</h3><p>Restate the question, final claim, major evidence chain, and most important limitations in your own words. Mark anything unread or still uncertain before deciding what to retain.</p></div></article></div></section>

<section class="component-section"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">After reading</p><h2>Choose what, if anything, should be retained</h2></div></div><div class="reading-closeout-basics"><article><h3>End without saving</h3><p>Use the reading to understand the paper in this conversation only.</p></article><article><h3>Keep a reading record</h3><p>Retain source-specific explanations, terminology, figures, and unresolved questions in the local knowledge service.</p></article></div><details class="reading-disclosure reading-closeout-disclosure"><summary>Possible proposed outputs</summary><div class="reading-disclosure-body reading-closeout-list"><article><strong>Source-backed knowledge candidate</strong><p>A narrow statement tied to this source, its evidence, and its limits.</p></article><article><strong>Study-specific application</strong><p>A judgement about how a named Study may use the source; it belongs in that Study's <code>04_knowledge/</code>.</p></article><article><strong>Experience or rule candidate</strong><p>A process lesson derived from reading; it needs separate review and cannot be promoted automatically.</p></article></div></details></section>

<section class="component-section"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">Storage boundary</p><h2>Keep the paper and derived records separate</h2></div></div><p>With source-specific permission, a reference manager such as Zotero may manage the bibliographic item and its PDF. The Knowledge service records which item and version were read; it is not a replacement PDF library. Reading records, knowledge candidates, Study applications, experience candidates, and formal rules have different owners and promotion steps.</p><details class="reading-storage-tree"><summary>Suggested local locations</summary><div><p><code>Knowledge/&lt;service-id&gt;/</code><span># managed local knowledge service</span></p><p class="reading-storage-child"><code>KNOWLEDGE_SERVICE_MANIFEST.yaml</code><span># owner, scope, and access boundary</span></p><p class="reading-storage-child"><code>&lt;service-defined-records&gt;/</code><span># reading records and source-backed knowledge</span></p><p class="reading-storage-child"><code>reference_manager/&lt;manager-owned-library&gt;/</code><span># a library owned by Zotero or another manager</span></p><p class="reading-storage-child"><code>&lt;Study&gt;/04_knowledge/</code><span># a named Study's application judgement</span></p><p class="reading-storage-child"><code>&lt;Study&gt;/05_memory/</code><span># Study-local experience and review trail</span></p></div></details></section>

<section class="component-section" aria-labelledby="reading-flow-title"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">How we read it together</p><h2 id="reading-flow-title">Move through it one layer at a time</h2></div></div><ol class="reading-flow"><li><span>01</span><div><h3>First identify the study's final conclusion</h3><p>Find the question, the authors' final answer, the evidence behind it, and where it applies. Understand the claim first without treating it as proved fact.</p></div></li><li><span>02</span><div><h3>Then understand the paper section by section</h3><p>What does each section do, which part of the question does it address, and how does it connect to the previous section and move toward the final conclusion?</p></div></li><li><span>03</span><div><h3>Then understand each section paragraph by paragraph</h3><p>Is the paragraph giving background, a definition, a method, a result, an interpretation, or a limitation? What evidence or reasoning does it use, and how does it advance the section?</p></div></li><li><span>04</span><div><h3>Read the key sentences last</h3><p>Break down only sentences that carry information: definitions, methodological conditions, numbers, comparisons, limitations, qualifications, and turns in reasoning.</p></div></li><li><span>05</span><div><h3>Read figures, tables, and supplements one by one</h3><p>Locate each item in the relevant paragraph, section, and overall claim. Then inspect titles, panels, axes, groups, colors, units, denominators, legends, and statistical marks, stating what it supports and what it cannot.</p></div></li><li><span>06</span><div><h3>Check whether the whole paper is understood</h3><p>Restate the question, final conclusion, main evidence chain, and most important limitations in your own words. Mark unread or uncertain material before deciding what to organize.</p></div></li></ol>
<details class="reading-disclosure"><summary>How closely do you want to read?</summary><div class="reading-disclosure-body reading-depth-list"><article><div><strong>Full close reading</strong><code>full_close_reading</code></div><p>Read every accessible main-text section, substantive paragraph, claim-bearing sentence, table, and figure. Include supplements only when they are available and inside the agreed scope.</p></article><article><div><strong>Structured close reading</strong><code>structured_close_reading</code></div><p>Read every substantive section and paragraph, reaching sentence level for definitions, methods, numbers, limitations, and reasoning turns.</p></article><article><div><strong>Focused reading</strong><code>focused_reading</code></div><p>Read only what you name, such as one method, Figure 2, a results paragraph, or the Discussion.</p></article></div></details>
<details class="reading-disclosure"><summary>What question do you want to bring to the reading?</summary><div class="reading-disclosure-body reading-lens-list"><p>Method learning, terminology, figure interpretation, critical appraisal, Journal Club, and reproducibility checks can be added during the sequence above.</p><ul><li>Why was this method used, and which assumptions make it valid?</li><li>How does this figure support the conclusion, and where might it be overinterpreted?</li><li>Does the reasoning from design to conclusion contain a gap, bias, or insufficient evidence?</li><li>What would another person need to reproduce or audit this work?</li></ul></div></details></section>

<section class="component-section" aria-labelledby="reading-closeout-title"><div class="section-header"><div><p class="section-context">After reading</p><h2 id="reading-closeout-title">Decide whether anything should be retained</h2></div></div><p class="reading-closeout-intro">First do a short closeout triage: identify what may be worth organizing, why, and what should not be retained. The reader still decides.</p><div class="reading-closeout-basics"><article><h3>End here</h3><p>The reading goal is complete and no record is needed.</p></article><article><h3>Keep a reading record</h3><p>Retain what you wanted to understand, what is now clear, what remains unclear, and a source pointer.</p></article></div><details class="reading-disclosure reading-closeout-disclosure"><summary>What else could this paper produce?</summary><div class="reading-disclosure-body reading-closeout-list"><article><strong>Source-backed knowledge card</strong><p>A narrow statement genuinely supported by this source, with its scope and limits.</p></article><article><strong>Glossary-term candidate</strong><p>Propose one only when the current glossary cannot explain a reusable concept.</p></article><article><strong>Knowledge-to-experience candidate</strong><p>If the lesson concerns the work process, separately review whether it is an experience; a knowledge card is not promoted automatically.</p></article><article><strong>Study application note</strong><p>Created only when a user names an existing Study and requests use there; the handoff contains a short note and source pointer.</p></article></div></details></section>
